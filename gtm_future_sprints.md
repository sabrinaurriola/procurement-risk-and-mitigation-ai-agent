# Future go-to-market sprints

> **Everything below is a plan and a set of hypotheses.** No user interviews, pilots, feedback or adoption numbers exist yet. The targets are what the next sprints are meant to test, not results.

**Starting point.** A working MVP that produces an evidence-backed risk and mitigation report for one commodity (sunflower oil), using fictional company data. The open questions are commercial: who has this problem badly enough to act, would they trust the output, and would they give us their own data?

---

## Sprint 1 — Problem discovery with procurement professionals

| | |
|---|---|
| **1. Goal** | Learn whether the problem is real, how procurement teams handle it today, and which part of the report they value (exposure figure, evidence, supplier leads, mitigation actions). |
| **2. Target user / buyer** | Raw-material buyers and procurement managers at small and mid-size food manufacturers in Spain (bakery, snacks, oils, canned food). Secondary: category managers at food distributors. |
| **3. Channel or motion** | Direct outreach on LinkedIn, Spanish food-industry associations and trade groups, and warm introductions. A 30-minute call in which the interviewee watches a 5-minute demo and critiques a sample report. |
| **4. Key deliverable** | A free sample report on a commodity the interviewee names, produced with the MVP, using the commodity benchmark and their description of a typical contract (not their real contracts). Output of the sprint: a one-page findings note. |
| **5. Success metric (hypothesis)** | 12 outreach conversations completed; at least 8 of the 12 describe a commodity-risk review they currently do by hand; at least 6 of the 12 say the sample report would change or speed up a decision they actually make. |

## Sprint 2 — Business validation: will they trust it with their own data?

| | |
|---|---|
| **1. Goal** | Test whether a buyer will supply real contract and forecast data, and whether the exposure figures are accurate enough for them to rely on after checking them against their own numbers. |
| **2. Target user / buyer** | The 3–5 most engaged people from Sprint 1, plus the person who signs off purchasing risk (head of procurement or finance director). |
| **3. Channel or motion** | Follow-up calls and a guided session: the buyer sends a small, anonymised contract and forecast file (a template is provided), the team loads it, and both review the output together. |
| **4. Key deliverable** | A personalised risk report on their data and a short checklist of every figure they could and could not reconcile. A written note on what would be needed for them to trust the output (sources, review step, audit trail). |
| **5. Success metric (hypothesis)** | At least 3 buyers provide anonymised data; at least 2 of them reconcile the baseline exposure figure within an agreed tolerance (to be set with each buyer before the session); at least 2 name a concrete decision where they would use the report. |

## Sprint 3 — Pilot adoption

| | |
|---|---|
| **1. Goal** | Find out whether one or two companies will use the tool on a recurring basis, and what they would pay for it. |
| **2. Target user / buyer** | One or two procurement teams that completed Sprint 2, with a named internal sponsor. |
| **3. Channel or motion** | A 6-week unpaid pilot with a weekly report for two commodities of the customer's choice, a fixed review call every two weeks, and an explicit decision meeting at the end. |
| **4. Key deliverable** | Weekly reports plus a pilot summary: reports delivered, decisions influenced, issues found. A one-page offer (scope, price range, support) tested at the final meeting. |
| **5. Success metric (hypothesis)** | At least 1 company completes the 6 weeks and opens at least 4 of the 6 weekly reports; the sponsor names at least 1 decision the reports informed; at least 1 company agrees to a follow-on paid or extended pilot or states a price they would pay. |

---

## Notes and constraints

- **Not a feature list.** Technical work (more commodities, public-risk monitoring of real suppliers, ERP integration) is only started if a sprint shows buyers want it. Public-risk monitoring of a customer's real suppliers is out of scope until a customer provides real supplier data.
- **Data handling.** Customer data would be anonymised on receipt and kept apart from the synthetic demonstration data. Nothing is sent to a pilot customer without their agreement.
- **Decision support only.** The agent never approves suppliers, places orders or changes contracts. Pilots keep a human reviewer in every step.
- **Metrics are hypotheses.** The numbers are starting targets chosen for small-sample learning; they should be revised after Sprint 1.
